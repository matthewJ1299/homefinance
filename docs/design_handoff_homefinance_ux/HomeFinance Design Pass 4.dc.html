Turn 6 · the missing items

The states between features

Approval, empty, offline, failure, notifications — then Goals, Settings, the front door, and the AI report. Nothing here is a new feature. It is the difference between software that works and software that feels finished.

Goals · one argument first

A "Saving up" category and a savings goal are currently two features doing one job. Car service — R400 a month, R3 600 sitting in it — is a savings goal, and once leftovers roll over it behaves like one with no extra machinery. Two places to look means two numbers that can disagree, and the one the user trusts is whichever they saw last.

The proposal

Savings goals become categories with a target. A category gains an optional target amount and date; when it has one, it renders as a goal. The Goals page becomes the long-horizon view of those same categories — progress, projection, what to change — rather than a parallel system.

Debt goals stay separate. A credit card payoff isn't an envelope you fill; it's a balance you reduce, with interest working against you. Different arithmetic, different screen, and the current credit-goal logic already handles it.

Why this wording

A split touches two people's balances. Half-applied is worse than not applied, so the message has to state that both sides rolled back — otherwise the honest reaction is to check Sydney's phone.

"Your details are still here" matters too. A failure that also loses what you typed is the one people don't forgive.

Try again must be idempotent. Retrying a split that partially wrote is how someone ends up owing R900 instead of R450.
